What we do

From the decision to get help, all the way to the destination.

Addiction isolates everyone it touches: the person who is sick, and the family watching it happen. The Lit Path steps into that gap. We help families reach their loved one, connect them with the right treatment facility, get them there safely, and stay with the family while treatment happens and after it ends.

01

Family reunification interventions

We bring the family together with their loved one in a guided group setting. The goal is not an ambush. It is to help someone hear, from people who love them and from people who have been where they are, that the life they have been living is a way to die and not a way to live.

  • Preparation with the whole family beforehand
  • Led by people with lived experience of addiction and recovery
  • A clear next step ready the moment they say yes

02

Treatment placement

Finding a bed should not be the hard part. Through our connections across the recovery community, we match your loved one with a treatment facility that fits their situation and work directly with that facility to secure their place.

  • Facility matching based on need, not availability alone
  • Direct coordination with admissions
  • Timing that works with the intervention, not against it

03

Sober companionship and safe transport

A sober companion with real experience of what that life is like stays with your loved one from decision to destination. That includes flying across the country if that is where the right facility is. Companions are trained in overdose response and relapse behavior, and they make sure the person is engaged and socializing, not withdrawing.

  • Door to door, start to finish
  • Trained in overdose response and relapse warning signs
  • Someone who knows the road because they have walked it

04

Family support, during treatment and after

While your loved one is being treated, you will have questions and worries. We stay available for all of them. Before they come home, we prepare you for what to expect from a newly sober person, how to hold boundaries, and which warning signs matter most.

  • Ongoing contact while treatment is underway
  • Preparation for the return home
  • Boundaries for mental health and for physical safety

How it works

Three steps, and we walk every one of them with you.

1

Call us

For families

When your loved one comes home.

Treatment ending is not the finish line. The first weeks home are often the hardest for the family, because a newly sober person is still fragile and the old patterns are still close. We prepare families for this before it happens, and we keep showing up after.

Two things we tell every family to watch for:

  • IsolationPulling away from the family, from meetings, from the people who helped. Withdrawal is often the first sign that something is slipping.
  • Suicidal behaviorTalk of hopelessness, giving things away, saying goodbye. Take it seriously every time. Do not wait to see if it passes.

If someone is in immediate danger, call 911. For a mental health or suicide crisis in the United States, call or text 988 any time, day or night.

What we help with

  • Helping the family reconnect with the sick member of their family in a group setting.
  • Identifying the behaviors of addiction, explained by people who have been there and made it to the other side.
  • Helping the family reassert boundaries with their loved one, for mental health and for physical safety.
  • Making sure the family is heavily supported with every question and concern while their loved one is being treated.
  • Preparing the family for the return home: what to expect, and what to watch for.

Why The Lit Path

People who have been there, and made it to the other side.

Everything we do is built on lived experience. The people who lead our interventions and travel as sober companions know what that life is like from the inside, because they lived it and got out. That is why they can say the things a family cannot, and be heard.

We will do everything it takes to show someone that being sober is the right choice, from people who reflect what they would one day hope to be.

We also know the recovery community. Facilities, admissions teams, sober living, meetings, and the people who run them. Those connections are how we get a bed, and how we get someone to it.
