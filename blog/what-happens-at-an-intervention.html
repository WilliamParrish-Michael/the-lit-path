What actually happens at an intervention

Most families picture an intervention the way television shows it: a surprise, a circle of chairs, a lot of crying, and a dramatic exit. Some of that happens. But the part that matters is the part the cameras skip, which is everything before the room and everything after it.

Before: the family does the work first

An intervention that is not prepared is just an argument with more people in it. So we start with the family, not the person who is sick. We talk through what you have been seeing: the lying, the missing money, the disappearing for days, the promises. We help you name those behaviors plainly, because the person you love will try to argue with vague worry and cannot argue with specifics.

We also prepare you for what they are likely to say. People who have lived in addiction know the scripts, because we used them ourselves. "I can stop on my own." "You are overreacting." "If you loved me you would not do this." Hearing those lines in advance, from someone who once said them, takes away most of their power.

And we line up the destination. Before anyone sits down, we have a treatment facility identified and a placement arranged. The worst outcome is a person saying yes and then having to wait three days for a bed. Yes has a short shelf life.

During: it is a conversation, not an ambush

The room is calm. Each family member speaks, briefly, about what they have seen and what they are afraid of. There is no yelling and no list of grievances. The point is not to shame someone into treatment. The point is for them to hear, from the people who love them most, that the life they have been living is a way to die and not a way to live.

Then someone who has been where they are talks to them. Not a lecture. A person who looks like what they might one day hope to be, saying: I know exactly what today feels like, and I am on the other side of it. That is often the moment the room changes.

We also help the family state boundaries. Not threats, boundaries. What you will and will not do going forward, for your own mental health and for everyone's physical safety. We help you say them in a way you can actually keep.

After: the moment they say yes

When someone says yes, we move. Bags are already packed. A sober companion is already there. The companion stays with them from that room all the way to the facility door, whether that is across town or across the country. The hours in between are the most dangerous hours of the whole process, and nobody goes through them alone.

Then we turn back to the family. While your loved one is in treatment you will have questions, second thoughts, and nights you cannot sleep. We stay reachable for all of it. And before they come home, we prepare you for what that looks like, which is a story of its own.

If they say no

Sometimes they say no. That is not the end. The boundaries the family stated still stand, and a no today is very often a yes in a week, once the consequences of no become real. We stay with the family through that too.
